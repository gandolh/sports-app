# Training science

Evidence review commissioned 2026-07-29 against the user's three stated goals: grow
muscle, improve posture, keep a healthy cardio level. Sources are systematic reviews,
meta-analyses and guideline bodies; the full source list is in the
[log entry](../log.md). **Read the "weak evidence" section before quoting any of this
in app copy.**

## Verdict on the three goals

| Goal | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Hypertrophy** | **Partly.** Real for pecs, triceps, front delt, quads, glutes, abs. **Not achievable** for lats, elbow flexors and grip; marginal for hamstrings and calves as written. |
| **Posture** | **Partly, and partly the wrong target.** Measured angles move. Evidence that *resting scapular position* changes is absent. Posture→health causation is weak. |
| **Cardio** | **Not achievable inside the session.** Achievable cheaply *outside* it for ~60–90s/day. |

## Load is not the problem — missing movement patterns are

Low loads (<60% 1RM) produce hypertrophy comparable to heavy loads, and 8 weeks of
push-ups matched 40% 1RM bench press for pec and triceps thickness. So bodyweight is
fine. **You cannot, however, hypertrophy a muscle whose joint action never appears in
the programme** — that is the actual limit here.

**Proximity to failure is what matters, and it matters *more* at low load** (effect
favouring failure roughly doubles below 50% 1RM). Prescribe **0–2 reps in reserve**.
"Stop at 12 because the ladder says so" with five reps left in the tank is a bug, not a
rule — the rep count should only be a trigger to advance the modifier.

### Muscles getting nothing, ranked by how much it matters

1. **Latissimus dorsi** (+ teres major, posterior delt under load) — no shoulder
   extension or adduction against resistance anywhere. The largest single hole.
2. **Elbow flexors** — zero elbow flexion. Push-ups measurably do nothing for biceps.
3. **Hamstrings** — a bodyweight hinge is limited by balance, not hamstring force.
4. **Plantar flexors** — nothing at all, and the cheapest gap to close.
5. **Rhomboids / mid + lower trapezius** — partial (see below), but almost no capacity
   for progressive overload.
6. **Lateral deltoid** — no overhead or abduction pattern.
7. **Grip / forearm flexors** — zero.
8. **Lumbar erector spinae** — minimal, and this one matters for posture.
9. **Hip adductors** — incidental only.

### What the postural pull slot is actually doing

Precise answer: **more than nothing, less than hoped.**

- **Mid/lower trapezius: a real stimulus.** Prone elevation variants hit 63–66% MVIC in
  mid trap, 42–48% in lower trap.
- **Latissimus dorsi: essentially nothing.** Y/T/W raises are scapular and glenohumeral
  *elevation*; the lat is never the prime mover and never loaded in extension.
- **Caveat that must not be dropped: EMG amplitude does not predict hypertrophy.**
  63% MVIC justifies "this muscle is working", not "this muscle will grow". No trial
  shows prone Y-T-W produces measurable mid-back hypertrophy.
- End-range isometrics are defensible — isometric training at long muscle lengths does
  produce hypertrophy above ~20% MVC — but isometric strength is strongly
  joint-angle-specific. It buys position-holding capacity, not pulling strength.

**The honest cost of no pull:** the largest back muscle, all elbow flexion, all grip,
and any progressive overload for the mid-back. Over 6–12 months that is a visibly and
functionally front-dominant physique. Prone Y-T-W does not substitute.

## The household-object finding

The zero-equipment constraint was interpreted as *no purchases*. Two options need no
purchase and close gaps 1, 2 and 7 outright:

- **A broom or mop handle across two chair backs / a bed frame / a counter edge** gives a
  **supine floor row**. Ladder: *feet forward, high row → feet further out (lower torso
  angle) → 3s eccentric → 2s top hold at full retraction → feet on a chair →
  single-arm assisted → archer row.*
- **A towel looped round a sofa or bed leg** — lower and far more available than a door.
  Ladder: *seated, knees bent → legs straighter → single-arm → single-arm 3s eccentric
  → lean further back.*
- **Heels hooked under a sofa** is a Nordic curl anchor — a *low* anchor, which is
  exactly what is available.

True zero-anchor fallbacks (self-resisted rows, prone towel-slide pull-downs, towel
curls against your own leg) are **mechanistically plausible with no training trials at
all.** App copy must not imply they replace pulling.

## Volume arithmetic

A 3-day rotation run daily = **2.33 sessions/week per slot** = **~7 direct sets/week per
pattern**. That is the "clearly working but sub-maximal" band: <5 sets is suboptimal,
~10+ is best. Frequency effects are negligible once volume is equated, so **add a 4th
set rather than more sessions** if more is wanted — at the cost of ~100s, which breaks
the 12-minute budget. A real trade-off, not a free win.

**Do not let the app compress rest below 60s to fit more work in.** Rest >60s carries a
small hypertrophy benefit, plateauing ~90s. 60s is the floor of the acceptable band, and
squeezing it is a net loss.

## Modifier limits

- **Tempo:** 0.5–8s per rep are equivalent; **>10s is inferior**. Cap eccentrics at ~5s.
  Super-slow is a downgrade, not a progression.
- **Range of motion:** progress at the **long** end only. Lengthened partials match or
  beat full ROM; shortened partials are clearly worse. Deficit push-up is valid; a
  top-half rep is not.
- **Unilateral / leverage:** the only modifiers that genuinely restore mechanical
  tension. These should be the primary progression axis, not tempo.

## Cardio needs its own dose

Guidelines converge on **150–300 min/week moderate or 75–150 vigorous**, plus strength
on ≥2 days. WHO 2020 **removed the 10-minute minimum bout**, which is what makes
micro-dosing legitimate rather than a workaround.

The programme accrues **effectively zero aerobic minutes**. Resistance training alone
raises VO2max only ~1.9 mL/kg/min, and at 7 sets/week expect near-nothing. This matters
more than the hypertrophy gap: cardiorespiratory fitness is the strongest single
mortality predictor with no observed upper limit, strength-only mortality benefit maxes
out at just 30–60 min/week (already reached here), and **strength + aerobic combined
gives 40% all-cause / 46% CVD reduction**. Marginal return on more strength is small;
marginal return on *any* aerobic work is large.

**It cannot fit inside a strength session** — as a circuit it forces rest below 60s,
makes sets fatigue-limited rather than effort-limited, and still yields the wrong kind
of vigorous minutes. Circuits are the weakest per minute for cardio fitness *and* the
most damaging to the strength stimulus. Hence cardio's own day.

Brisk walking for guideline minutes remains outside the app's scope and is not
substitutable by anything in it.

## Concurrent training — the ordering is what matters

*Reviewed 2026-07-29 to place the cardio day in the rotation.*

Interference is **maximised when HIIT at 95–100% VO2max runs alongside resistance work
at ≥10RM** — which is exactly hard intervals plus 5–12-rep bodyweight sets. This
programme sits squarely in the risk zone, so placement is not cosmetic.

The mitigation the literature actually supports is order, not distance: **strength
first, conditioning after.** Same-session work needs ≥6h separation for the AMPK/mTOR
pathways not to compete; across adjacent days, `Legs → Cardio → Push` is the correct
direction and `Cardio → Legs` is the wrong one.

**Frequency is not the lever; volume is.** The dose-response meta-regressions find
frequency's effect on hypertrophy compatible with negligible once weekly volume is
matched. So there is nothing to gain from training legs more often — only from hitting
5–9 direct sets per pattern per week with correct separation. With daily training you
cannot avoid cardio landing adjacent to legs unless legs days go back-to-back, which
breaks 48-hour recovery; the achievable optimum is therefore the order alone.

## Isometric holds have a ceiling, and it is low

*Reviewed 2026-07-29 to set the per-rung caps in [decisions.md](decisions.md#hold-durations-are-capped-by-evidence-not-by-symmetry).*

- **McGill programs 10-second holds in a reverse pyramid**, not long holds — the goal
  is priming stability, not exhausting the muscle. Two of the Big 3 are isometric by
  design, which is why the holds were kept rather than converted to reps.
- **Past 60s transfer drops sharply; past ~2 minutes it is meaningless or harmful** —
  most people are no longer training the core, only practising discomfort while form
  degrades. Population norms are ~106s (women) / ~118s (men), so a 60s cap is not a
  low bar.
- **Scapular work is prescribed at 5–10s per position**, beginners 10s building to 30s,
  with longer variants at 30–60s. ACE's prone scapular series is short holds with
  multiple reps, performed daily — which is what justifies the daily posture block.
- The tuck L-sit cap is set by **the wrists**, not the abdominals.

## Posture: strengthening beats stretching

Contrary to a very commonly repeated claim, **thoracic mobility and hip-flexor work are
NOT higher-leverage than upper-back strengthening.** In the hyperkyphosis literature
targeted strengthening produced a large pooled effect while stretching alone was
equivocal. Hip-flexor stretching moved anterior pelvic tilt by **1.2°** — not an
intervention.

**Add spinal extensor strengthening.** It is the best-supported single lever and is
currently absent from the programme. Treat mobility work as a low-cost extra, not a
priority.

## Where the evidence is weak — read before writing app copy

- **Self-resistance and friction-based pull substitutes: no training literature at all.**
- **EMG %MVIC does not predict hypertrophy.**
- **The postural literature is weak throughout** — I² 75–80%, low study quality, short
  follow-up, no clinical-meaningfulness analysis, and explicit *absence* of evidence that
  exercise changes resting scapular position.
- **Posture→pain causation is unestablished** and age-confounded. Position posture as
  appearance and comfort, **not** as a health outcome.
- **Exercise snacks rest entirely on VO2max, a surrogate.** No trial shows they reduce
  events or mortality; samples are small.
- **Low-load hypertrophy trials are ≤12 weeks, mostly untrained.** They show bodyweight
  training works; they say nothing about month 18. Expect a real ceiling in push and
  squat.
- **"10 sets per muscle per week" is a convention** from between-study meta-regression,
  not a demonstrated threshold. ~7 sets is "adequate", not "deficient".
- **The ladder-plus-modifier scheme itself is untested.** Every ingredient is evidenced;
  the recipe has never been trialled against load progression. This is the largest
  untested assumption in the product, and it is worth watching for stalls in-app.
