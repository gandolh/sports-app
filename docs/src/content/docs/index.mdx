**sports-app** is a zero-equipment calisthenics trainer, installed as an
offline-first PWA. You open it, it already knows what today's session is, you
pick how hard a day you are having, and you work through the exercises one page
at a time tapping Next. It takes about twelve minutes. It never asks you
anything else.

## The schedule never adapts to what you log

Since 2026-09-04 the app records what you did, shows dates, a streak and a
calendar, and compares against last time. None of it reaches the prescription.
It grows on a **fixed schedule** — roughly one ladder rung every six weeks — as a
pure function of sessions completed.

That is the governing design decision, and it is what makes everything else
small. A schedule that adapted would need a model of you, a way to be wrong about
you, and a way to tell you it had changed its mind. This one needs a count of
sessions.

:::note[`SPEC.md` is partly superseded]
The v1 design document describes the adaptive engine this app no longer has.
Where it disagrees with the corpus, **the corpus wins** until SPEC.md is revised.
:::

## The constraints that shaped everything

- **Zero equipment, no purchases.** A hard constraint, reaffirmed three times.
  Its consequence is stated openly rather than hidden: the app cannot train
  pulling *strength*, only scapular retraction and upper-back endurance.
- **Sweaty hands, phone on the floor.** Mid-workout every tap is expensive. This
  is why the numerals are oversized. The pure core in `client/src/domain/` still
  never reads the clock.
- **One user, then two.** More than one person can train on the same deployment,
  each signed in through Ward. The product is still shaped by one person's
  constraints, not by a market.
